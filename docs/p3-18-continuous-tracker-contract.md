# P3-18 continuous foothold Tracker — implementation contract

This is a new task contract, not a resume or changed score for a1_directed_jump_v5. The old all-four-flight,3cm-per-hop launch gate and inter-hop forced0.3s preparation/0.2s stable hold remain in legacy regression only. A1 actuator and collision limits remain unchanged. No claim of an implemented/trained new policy yet.

Environment: oriented_shared_surfaces_v1 shared cuboids; selected contacts represented in surface-local coordinates with inward2cm foot-center margin and normal offset. A foot name belongs to a planned contact, never to the terrain surface. Multiple feet may use the same surface at different or overlapping times. Start and final flat platforms are initially allowed; intermediate targets may have roll/pitch/yaw and height/lateral displacement. Catch-floor contact is failure. Per-foot forces project onto selected normals; tangential motion and impact are surface-relative.

Planner/teacher contact buffer: finite sequence of per-group paired left/right XYZ targets, surface identifiers/normals, target heading and final-stop flag. Initial calibrated stance establishes target0. Intermediate rear targets reuse the earlier front targets; the final shared platform uses separate front/rear contact locations to allow a physical four-foot stance rather than asking four feet to occupy two points. Front pair begins towardtarget1; rear pair tracks the front pair's previous accepted target. At least current+next target per group is observable, with mask and age. This finite scripted buffer is a Tracker-training input; it must not be called autonomous navigation. The subsequent Planner must generate and replace it from map+goal and validate it with the same policy in physics rollout.

Progress prototype: src/parkour/pair_target_progress.py. Front/rear pair acceptance and target changes are asynchronous. Front accepted-target events allow the rear to advance to the corresponding past target; each group retains its own clock and contiguous-contact counters. No simulator reset, velocity zeroing, joint hold or global stabilization is requested at intermediate transitions. Progress accepts externally validated per-foot contacts, not raw force bits. Initial baseline chooses BOTH left/right contacts sustained60ms; EITHER is a separate explicit option, and the choice must be recorded. The reference paper's either-foot updater is not silently claimed identical to our both-foot baseline. All per-foot misses remain metrics. Final accepted indices alone are not episode success: final platform support, goal tolerance and final stabilization are additionally required.

Observation candidate: proprioception with clearly marked oracle velocity baseline, current/next contact XYZ in body coordinates, corresponding normals, front/rear target age, future-target validity, final-stop flags, previous actions and measured contact state. Fix exact dimensions before training and store normalization/checkpoint contract. Old66-input weights cannot be blindly restored into this task. A new policy or an explicit audited initialization is required; no forced zero-history interpretation.

Reward design work remains: once-only valid-contact progression, distance-to-current-contact shaping, torso progress toward feasible next support, torque/joint limits/action changes, surface-tangent slip and normal impact. Apply stop penalty only at the final target. Avoid dense rewards that favor sitting at one accepted target indefinitely; episode time budget and new-target rewards must be checked with zero/static baselines. Flight is measured where it occurs and required for designated gap maneuvers, not enforced at every ordinary step. No artificial base forces or velocity injection count as policy execution.

Next implementation: actual surface/target validity tensors + new DirectRLEnv, isolated smoke with matching target/normal/clock bookkeeping, then easy shared-surface course learning with nonzero height/slope/yaw axes from the outset. The demanding mixed geometry preview is a design/stress scene, not the initial random-policy curriculum or an assumed feasible path. Evaluate course completion and stops at intermediate surfaces, then expand difficulty based on observed capability. Do not continue a long sequence of isolated straight-jump reward sweeps as a prerequisite.
